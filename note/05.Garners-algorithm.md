# Garner's algorithm

Consider the following system of congruences (modulo coprime):

### 1. Problem setting
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{cases}
x \equiv a_1 ~ (\bmod m_1) \\
x \equiv a_2 ~ (\bmod m_2) \\
x \equiv a_3 ~ (\bmod m_3) \\
\vdots\\
x \equiv a_k = (\bmod m_k)
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem (CRT), a solution x exists uniquely in 

$$
\begin{aligned}
x ~ (\bmod M), \quad M = m_1 m_2 ... m_k
\end{aligned}
$$

### 2. Idea : Mixed Radix Representation

Expands $$x$$ to the following form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x ~ = ~ c_1 ​+ c_2 ​m_1 ​+ c_3 m_1 ​m_2 ​+ \cdots + c_k ​m_1 ​m_2​ \cdots m_{k−1}
\end{aligned}
$$

- Each coefficient $$c_i$$ can be chosen to fall within the range of modulo $$m_i$$

### 3. How to find the coefficients
#### Step 1: First coefficient

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1 = a_1
\end{aligned}
$$

because:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x \equiv c_1 \equiv a_1  ~ (\bmod m_1)
\end{aligned}
$$

#### Step 2: Second coefficient

Next, the remainder when divide 

$$
\begin{aligned}
x ~ = ~ c_1 ​+ c_2 ​m_1 ​+ c_3​ m_1 ​m_2 ​+ \cdots
\end{aligned}
$$

by $$m_2$$ will be:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1​ + c_2 ​m_1 \equiv a_2​ ~ (\bmod m_2​)
\end{aligned}
$$

Rearrange the equation for $$c_2$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_2 \equiv (a_2 - c_1​)m_1^{-1}​ ~ (\bmod m_2​)
\end{aligned}
$$

#### Step 3: Third coefficient

Similally, divide $$x$$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x ~ = ~ c_1 ​+ c_2 ​m_1 ​+ c_3​ m_1 ​m_2 ​+ c_4 m_1 m_2 m_3 ​+ \cdots
\end{aligned}
$$

by $$m_3$$ will be:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1​ + c_2 ​m_1  ​+ c_3​ m_1 ​m_2 \equiv a_3​ ~ (\bmod m_3​)
\end{aligned}
$$

Rearrange the equation for $$c_3$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_3 \equiv (a_3 - (c_1 + c_2 m_1)) \cdot (m_1 m_2)^{-1}​ ~ (\bmod m_3​)
\end{aligned}
$$

#### General form

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_j \equiv (a_j - \sum_{i=1}^{j-1} c_i \prod_{l=1}^{i-1} m_l) \cdot (\prod_{l=1}^{j-1} m_l)^{-1} ~ (\bmod m_j)
\end{aligned}
$$

### 4.Algorithm procedure (summary)
#### 4.1 Input : 
- surplus $$(a_1, \dots , a_k)$$
- modulo $$(m_1, \dots , m_k)$$ (coprime)

#### 4.2 Initialize : 
- initialize: $$c_1 = a_1$$

#### 4.3 Loop :
- for each $$j = 2 \dots k$$
  - calculate partial sum s: $$s = c_1 + c_2 m_1 + \cdot + c_{j-1} \prod_{l=1}^{j-2} m_l$$
  - difference: $$d = (a_j - s ) (\bmod m_j)$$
  - calculate the inverse inv: $$inv = (\prod_{l=1}^{j-1} m_l)^{-1} ~ (\bmod m_j)$$
  - coefficient $$c_j = d \cdot inv ~ (\bmod m_j)$$

#### 4.4 Finally :
- we get $$x$$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x ~ = ~ c_1 ​+ c_2 ​m_1 ​+ c_3 m_1 ​m_2 ​+ \cdots + c_k ​m_1 ​m_2​ \cdots m_{k−1}
\end{aligned}
$$
